- Precision vs. recall, correct definitions after two rounds of self-correction: precision = TP/(TP+FP), penalizes **false positives**; recall = TP/(TP+FN), penalizes **false negatives**. These are genuinely different optimization targets, not just "two accuracy-like numbers."
  - Recall-priority example: illness screening on a rare-positive-class dataset. Missing an actual sick patient (false negative) is the costlier mistake, so you tune for high recall (catch nearly all true positives) even at the cost of more false positives (unnecessary follow-ups).
  - Precision-priority example: a smart pet door trained to recognize only your specific cat. A false positive (an intruder cat gets let in) matters more than a false negative (your own cat occasionally has to wait), so you tune for high precision.
  - Concrete case where low loss doesn't mean a model is good: on a heavily imbalanced dataset (99% healthy, 1% sick), a model that always predicts "healthy" achieves very low average loss and high accuracy, it's right 99% of the time, but recall on the sick class is 0%, it never catches a single true case. Loss/accuracy alone can hide a metric that actually matters for the task.
- Learning rate schedulers, why the learning rate should change over training rather than stay fixed: large steps early make fast progress toward the ballpark of the optimum without wasting time; smaller steps later avoid overshooting/oscillating around the minimum and let the model fine-tune toward higher validation accuracy. Two distinct scheduler types matter here, and they work differently:
  - **StepLR**: decays the learning rate by a fixed factor (`gamma`) at fixed, predetermined intervals (every `step_size` epochs), on a schedule set in advance. It has no awareness of how training is actually going, it just ticks down on a clock.
  - **ReduceLROnPlateau**: the actually adaptive one, monitors a metric (e.g. validation loss) and only cuts the learning rate once that metric stops improving for a set number of epochs. This reacts to training progress rather than following a fixed timetable.
