## Kaggle Titanic - 10 MLflow experiments (catch-up item)

Ran the 10-variant sweep (3 logistic regression, 4 random forest, 3 gradient boosting), all logged to MLflow, and compared in the UI with the parallel-coordinates plot.

- Best run: RandomForestClassifier, n_estimators=300, max_depth=None. Run id 0f0ae7986f684ac1b2685778157802f1. Accuracy ~0.810.
- What seemed to matter: within the gradient-boosting runs, the learning rate (a good rate gave the best of those), and for random forest, more n_estimators trended slightly better.

Honest caveat (noted for next time): the spread across all 10 runs was small (~0.793 to ~0.810 on a ~180-row test set), so most of that gap is within noise - sub-percent differences are not reliable signal here. Also learning_rate is a gradient-boosting-only parameter (not on the RF winner); the parallel-coords plot pools all model families onto shared axes, which is why it reads as one axis. Takeaway: the workflow (track -> compare -> pick + justify) is the point, and the honest read is "these models are roughly equivalent on Titanic; pick the simplest that does well."
